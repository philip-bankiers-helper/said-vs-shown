# Editorial rules — what an episode may say and show

## Story shape (every story, every edition)

1. **Receipt beat:** what happened, said once, with the real source on screen and the sentence highlighted as it is spoken. Add a bespoke teaching illustration when it helps, tagged ILLUSTRATION (and "not a <product> output" when a product is involved).
2. **Boundary beat:** PROVES / DOESN'T PROVE (or "Establishes / Doesn't establish", "Shows / Doesn't show"), split by the dashed amber evidence line. When the source states its own caveat, show it as a second receipt.
3. **Board** between stories: the finished story's TEST slot fills, and the camera pushes into the next card.

Close with:
- the **payoff**: claim ↔ the test that fits it, built row by row;
- **one thing**: a question the viewer can use this week;
- the **close**: a receipt-style card with sources, computed counts and the disclosure line, held for about 7 s of silence.

## Badge rule (it grades the HEADLINE claim)

- **Checked:** the headline fact is directly visible in a primary source (a published artifact, a dated release entry, a signed agreement, an official record).
- **Company:** the headline rests on the company's or authors' own account of capability, performance or results (benchmarks they ran, features they describe, funding they announce).
- **Reported:** secondary reporting only.
- **Provider:** the discovery provider reported it, and no primary source was found.
- **Older:** real, but outside the episode's window.

## Wording rules (each one learned from a refuter catch)

- **Attribute provenance claims:** "manuscripts **it says were** produced by…", "the authors **report**…".
- **Scheduled is not available:** write "Free and Go **scheduled** from Oct 8".
- **Name plans and tiers exactly;** don't summarize them as "paid users".
- **Benchmarks:** say "top model **at launch**" when intervals overlap. Never "AI is X% as smart as people".
- **Unstable numbers:** never narrate a number the brief marks unstable (for example, moving repo counts). Round in the voice, keep it exact on screen.
- **Don't borrow the brief's wording** when the sources don't contain it.
- **"Doesn't prove" items** must be things a reasonable viewer might wrongly infer, not strawmen.
- **Never present an illustration as a product's real output.**

## Pace and captions

- **Pace:** 145–185 wpm (target ~160), one idea per sentence. Contractions are welcome; hype words are not.
- **Captions:** phrase-level, ≤42 characters, chunked for ≤19 cps (the gate is 21: the margin absorbs ASR timing jitter), never across a scene cut, ≥0.5 s where the scene allows (a shorter cue merges with a neighbour, or starts earlier, back to its own scene cut; a one-word scene with a short gap after it can leave a cue under 0.5 s).
- **Numbers:** spoken numbers show as digits.
